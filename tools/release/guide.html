Player's Guide

Ogre Battle Gaiden: Prince of Zenobia, English fan translation (Neo Geo Pocket Color), with the Windows player zenobia.exe

The original game came with a manual in Japanese only. This guide explains the player program, how the game works, and the things the game never tells you outright: class changes, recruits, routes, endings and the secrets after the ending. Story details sit in sections marked as spoilers, folded shut until you open them.

1. Getting started with the player

Opening your game

Keep zenobia.exe and ogre_en.bps together in a folder of your own, such as Documents\Zenobia. The player stores its settings, saves and save states next to itself, so avoid write-protected folders like Program Files.

Start zenobia.exe and choose your copy of the Japanese game with File > Open game (Ctrl+O), or drop the file onto the window. The .ngc file works, and so does the .zip it came in. The player translates the game in memory while it loads; your own file is never changed. The next time you start the player, the same game opens by itself.

To play the English version somewhere else, such as another emulator or a flash cart, use File > Save English ROM (Ctrl+S). It writes a separate English .ngc file and checks it after writing.

Saving: game saves, states and continuing

There are three ways to keep your progress, and they work differently.

The game's own saves

On the scenario map (the map of the whole land between chapters), press B to open the popup, choose Data, then Save. The game has three save files. To play a saved game, choose Load on the title screen. The player writes these saves to saves\<game file name>.sav a couple of seconds after the game saves, and again when you quit; the previous file is kept as a .bak copy.

During a chapter you cannot use Data. Use Suspend on the command bar instead: it saves the game in progress, and you resume it with Continue on the title screen. The suspend data is erased once you resume. After suspending, the game stays on its last message; press F12 to reset, or quit the player.

Save states

F5 saves the exact moment and F9 loads it, at any time, even in the middle of a battle. There are ten slots; F6 and F7 change the slot, and the title bar shows the current one. States are stored in the states folder. A state made with a different version of the game or patch asks before loading, because it may not work.

Continue where you left off

When you quit, the player keeps the moment you stopped at. At the next start it asks whether to continue from there; Options > When the player starts changes this, and File > Continue where I left off does it at any time.

Save states are convenient, but keep using the game's own saves too. The chapter saves and the import command work with game saves. A save state records the whole machine and may not load correctly with another version of the patch, while a game save holds only the game's own record of your army, money and progress.

2. How the game works

Starting a new game

The title screen offers NewGame, Load and Continue. Load and Continue can only be chosen when saved or suspended data exists. NewGame first asks which of the three files to save to.

Warren, an astrologer, then gives you a tarot reading. You name the hero (up to 8 letters; the default is Tristan), answer six questions, and draw a single card. The reading decides the hero's starting stats, and also some of the volunteers who join the army at the start of chapter 2. After it, the scenario map titled "The Zenobian Age" appears.

In chapters 1 and 2 the game offers seven "Game System Tips", short lessons on the basics. They are worth hearing once.

Real time, pausing, day and night

On the map of a chapter, everything happens in real time: your units and the enemy's move at once, and time flows from morning to night and back. Press Option to stop time while you think, and Option again to let it run. Time also stops while a menu, a window or the movement flags are open, and during battles.

How long a day lasts depends on the game speed set in Monitor: about 7 minutes of play at speed 1, a little under 5 minutes at speed 2 (the setting a new game starts with) and about 2.5 minutes at speed 3. Each new day you collect income and pay costs (see money). The colors of the map change with the hour. Some events happen only by day or only at night, and the items Scent of Night and Light's Whisper turn the time to night or day at once.

Units and leaders

Your volunteer army is made of units. A unit holds up to five characters, and a large character takes two places. The army can have up to 100 characters and up to 25 units. Characters who are in no unit wait in the stock.

The first character of a unit is its leader. Not everyone can lead: the status screen shows Leader: OK or Leader: No. The leader of the first unit is the commander of the whole army (Estrada in chapter 1, the hero from chapter 2 on), and that leader cannot be changed.

Within a unit, characters stand in a front row and a back row. Each class has one attack it uses from the front and another from the back; on the status screen, F: and B: show these attacks and how many times the character attacks with each.

The Organize screen (from the scenario map popup, or from the command bar during a chapter) has these commands: Character (View status, Dismiss, Class change), Add character, Remove, Swap units, Formation, Change leader, and Items. During a chapter, units already in the field cannot be changed there; use the Organize command in the unit's own window instead, which offers Formation, Dismiss, Class change and, when the leader is not a story character, Change leader. Story characters cannot be dismissed.

Headquarters and deploying units

Each chapter has a headquarters (HQ) where your army starts. In chapter 1 you have a single unit. From chapter 2 on, the command bar has Deploy unit, which sends a waiting unit out of the HQ. Deploying costs goth, and the game tells you the price first.

At most seven of your units can be on the map at the same time, the first unit included. With seven out, the command bar has no Deploy unit.

When any unit except the first ends its move on the HQ, the game asks whether to recall it. The item Return Half also sends a unit back to the HQ at once.

Moving units

Put the cursor on one of your units (or on the HQ) and press A. A small menu shows NEXT, the units standing there and the place itself. Choose a unit and press A to open its window, with these commands:

With Move you place up to three flags with A. Pressing A again on the last flag confirms the route (a third flag confirms it by itself); B removes the last flag. Units walk in straight lines from flag to flag, so use flags to steer around water; mountains can be crossed, but slowly. Holding B while you move the cursor makes it faster.

Pressing A on empty ground opens the command bar: NEXT, Deploy unit (from chapter 2), Organize, Items, Tarot cards, Finances, Monitor and Suspend. NEXT lists your units and the known towns; pick one and the cursor jumps there, which is the quickest way to find a unit on the move.

Towns, churches and hidden places

Towns and churches are strongholds. Send a unit into one to liberate it; places held by the enemy have to be won in battle first. Liberating a stronghold can bring information, items or a tarot card, and every liberated stronghold sends you money each day. Without money you cannot deploy units, so liberate as you go.

When you liberate a place, the game offers to draw a tarot card. The card changes some of your characters' stats right away (a message says which), and you can keep it for battle. A unit standing in a stronghold slowly gets its HP back, and visiting a town again sometimes brings new information.

Put the cursor on a town and choose the place icon to see its window: population, two ratings shown as LOW, MID or HIGH, and its daily income.

Recruiting

From chapter 2 on, a unit standing in a town can Recruit. What you can hire depends on the class of the unit's leader (see the recruit table), and recruits arrive two levels below the leader. The unit needs room for the newcomer, and the army must have room too.

A unit led by a Necromancer or a Lich can also recruit out in the field, away from towns and churches, although the game's help text says towns only.

Churches

A character whose HP falls to 0 can no longer fight. In a church, the unit window offers Rite of revival, which brings fallen characters back for a fee. The item Soul Call revives one character.

Hidden towns and churches

Many towns, and a few churches, are hidden. A hidden place appears when one of your units walks onto its spot, and the item Crystal Ball reveals hidden towns and churches. Villagers drop hints, such as an island of just the right size or a mountain road that ends for no reason. The island town of Orata in the Gross Region can be reached by a flying unit.

Buried treasure

Some spots on the map hide buried treasure; a unit walking over one finds it. The item Dowsing Rod uncovers one treasure. The item you get is picked at random among weapons, armor and accessories.

Money: income and expenses

The currency is goth. At the start of each new day the Income & Expenses window shows the aid paid by your liberated strongholds, the deployment cost of your units in the field, the money carried over, and your total funds. Finances on the command bar shows the same forecast at any time. If you cannot pay, the game makes you recall characters from your deployed units; choosing a unit's leader recalls the whole unit.

Clearing a chapter pays a bonus. Selling items and gifts from towns bring in more.

Battles

When one of your units meets an enemy unit or neutral monsters on the map, a battle starts. You do not control each character: the fight plays out by itself, and each character attacks according to its class and row. What you choose is the unit's tactics:

TacticEffect
Show guts!Balanced tactics for offense and defense.
Spread out!Targets the characters with the most HP.
Get the leader!Targets the enemy leader.
Finish them!Targets the characters with the least HP.

Set them with Tactics in the unit window, or during a battle. Press A in a battle to pause it and open the battle command bar: Tarot cards (use a card from your stock), Tactics, Retreat (pull the unit out of the fight), Animation (battle animations on or off), and, against neutral monsters, Persuade.

Defeating the enemy leader wins the battle at once. If time runs out first, the result depends on the opponent. Against an enemy unit, the enemy is the winner and your unit is pushed back on the map. Against neutral monsters, the game judges how the fight went, and it can end in a draw.

Characters gain experience in battle. Experience counts up to 100, and characters who reach it gain a level when the battle ends ("LEVEL UP!").

If the leader of any unit but the first falls, that unit turns into a ghost and walks away. The game will not deploy it again while its leader cannot fight.

Neutral monsters and persuasion

On their way across the map, your units sometimes run into neutral monsters. Every area has them except Estrada's Manor (chapter 1) and the Nessa Region (chapter 7). The kind of monster depends on the terrain where the meeting happens. Outside the Free Battle Areas each kind has a fixed level, from 3 in the Gross Region up to 24 at Laika Lakeside. In the Free Battle Areas their level is the level of your unit's leader plus 6. The item Echo Chime calls one of the area's kinds at random, whatever the terrain.

Persuade tries to win them over; your chance depends mostly on the charisma (CHA) and luck (LUK) of your leader against the monster's charisma. A persuaded monster joins your stock, so add it to a unit afterward with Organize.

Items

Items on the command bar and Items on the Organize screen both offer Use item, Equip item, Remove item, Sell item, Discard item, Sort and Tidy up. Press Option on an item to read what it does. Each character can equip one item, and any class can equip any item. Your stock holds up to 60 different items, and up to 99 of each.

Some items worth knowing:

  • Hero's Shard raises a character's level by one. Life Source, Sword Symbol, Speed Gear, Magus Paper, Heart of Leo, Mirror of Heart and Vessel of Fate raise max HP, STR, AGI, INT, CHA, ALI and LUK by a few points.
  • Chaos Up and Chaos Down raise or lower the Chaos Frame by 5.
  • 7-League Boots move a unit at once to a town you have liberated.
  • Echo Chime calls neutral monsters to fight; Joker Card gives a random tarot card; Crystal Ball reveals hidden places; Dowsing Rod finds buried treasure.
  • Items described as "Use in versus battles" work only in link-cable battles.

How you lose

The game is over in two cases:

  • The leader of your first unit falls in battle: Estrada in chapter 1, the hero after that.
  • An enemy unit takes your headquarters.

In some chapters enemy units march on your headquarters. Keep a strong unit there: a unit that loses a battle is pushed back, and an enemy that walks into an empty HQ takes it. Losing any other town to the enemy lowers your Chaos Frame by 5.

Clearing a chapter and going back

Each chapter has a goal, usually an enemy leader in a castle or town; the opening scenes tell you where. Reaching it ends the chapter. The "Congratulation!" screen shows your bonus money, and the scenario map opens the next area.

Some areas you have cleared stay on the scenario map for a while, and you can go back to them to train, find hidden places or finish errands. In a cleared area there are no enemy units, no daily aid and no deployment cost, and the command bar gets Return to map to leave whenever you like.

The Chaos Frame and alignment

The Chaos Frame (0 to 100) is your reputation: it shows how much the people support what you do. The cheats menu calls it reputation. Your final score appears on the results screen after the ending. These things move it:

  • Liberating a town. The change depends on the alignment of the unit that does it, compared with the town; a unit of low alignment lowers the Frame, and the leader's class matters too.
  • Losing a town to the enemy: minus 5.
  • Many answers and choices in conversations, and some purchases.
  • Tarot cards when you draw them, and the items Chaos Up and Chaos Down.

Alignment (ALI, 0 to 100) belongs to each character. It limits the classes a character can change to (see the class table), and the Sun card damages everyone with low alignment. Some conversation choices change the hero's alignment.

3. Characters and class changes

Character stats

The status screen (Organize, Character, View status) shows a character's class, level (Lv), HP, and six stats: STR (strength), AGI (agility), INT (intelligence), CHA (charisma), ALI (alignment) and LUK (luck). It also shows the character's cost in goth, the experience needed for the next level, the front-row and back-row attacks, the movement type (MOVE) and the equipped item.

How class changes work

Choose Class change on the Organize screen (Character, Class change) or in a unit's Organize command. Each member is marked OK! (can change class), UP OK! (can move up to a higher class) or BAD (no class available). Choose a member to see the classes open to them.

A character can take a class when they meet its requirements: a minimum level, an alignment range and a minimum charisma. The tables below list them. Characters of the Fighter line can always go back to Fighter (except the Lich), and characters of the Amazon line to Amazon. Other changes back down a line still need the requirements of the class you change to. A unit leader can only change to a class that can lead. Story characters with classes of their own, such as the hero and Kain, have no class changes in this list.

The hero's class

The hero and Kain have classes of their own that the class change list never touches. The hero starts as a Fighter, becomes a Knight when Estrada knights him at the start of chapter 2, and becomes a General after the events in Estoa that lead into chapter 7. On the Megaholten route, the hero becomes a Lord at the start of chapter 12 if Yorkleif and Grant are both in the army.

4. Routes and side areas

This section tells you where the story branches and what each choice gives, without the story itself.

The order of the chapters

  1. Chapters 1 to 6 come in a fixed order.
  2. After chapter 6, choose Lichtfloss on the scenario map. If your Chaos Frame is 65 or more, the king asks you to deal with giants on the Ein Peninsula.
  3. Then comes a visit to Estoa (Estrada's Manor), which opens chapter 7.
  4. After chapter 7, choose the Norodom Region (chapter 8) or Megaholten (chapter 9).
  5. Both routes pass through chapter 10, go on to chapter 11 or 12 depending on the route, and end in the Final Chapter.

7. After the ending

When the last scene of the ending is over, and before the staff roll starts, the game saves your finished campaign to the file you were playing. After the results screen the game returns to the title screen; choose Load and that file to continue. The scenario map now offers the three Free Battle Areas, and many areas you visited before are open again, depending on your route and choices.

Playing cleared areas

The areas you have cleared, and the Free Battle Areas, follow the rules of a cleared area: no enemy units, no daily aid, no deployment cost, and Return to map on the command bar. Neutral monsters still roam, so these areas are good for training. A side area that you opened but never cleared still plays as a normal chapter.

The Free Battle Areas and win streaks

The three Free Battle Areas have no story events. Their shops sell items found in no other shop, such as Chaos Up and Chaos Down (see the shop table). The game counts the battles you win there in a row. A fight with neutral monsters that you do not win, in any area, resets the count to zero; that includes a retreat and a draw. The count gives these rewards:

Battles won in a rowWhat happens
10If the 10th win is against ordinary neutral monsters and you defeat their leader, you receive Yuria's Letter (for Gilbert).
20The next encounter is a unit led by a Gungnir. Defeat its leader and a Gungnir joins you.
30The next encounter is a unit led by a Trooper. Defeat its leader and a Trooper joins you.
40The next encounter is the Lord (unless he is already in your army). Defeat him and he joins you.
More than 30Deneb may appear now and then. Defeat her and she joins you.
Any other countCanopus and Ares appear rarely. Defeat them and they join (Canopus only if Gilbert is in your army).

The special units are met on the map like other neutral monsters, at random while your units travel, and their level follows the same rule (your leader's level plus 6). The Echo Chime calls only ordinary monsters. Special units appear only while your army has room for another character, so keep places free: if the army is full when the count is exactly 20, 30 or 40, an ordinary encounter takes the special one's place.

Defeating the leader of ordinary neutral monsters led by a Firbolg in a Free Battle Area gives a Cat's Tongue. Of the three Free Battle Areas, only Area 2 has Firbolgs. They turn up there over shallow water (a flying unit can go there), or when an Echo Chime used there happens to call them.

In Free Battle Area 1, the church in the north-west of the map gives the Bond to Future to a unit that contains the Lord, Lanselot, Warren, Gilbert and Canopus together. It is a battle item for link-cable battles.

8. Chapter saves, cheats and the console date

Chapter saves

The Chapter saves folder next to zenobia.exe holds ready-made game saves: one at the start of each chapter, and one on each side of the choices that change the route. Use them to jump back to a part of the story you remember, or to try the other route. In each one the hero is named Tristan and the game is in file 1.

  1. Open your game in the player.
  2. Choose File > Import saved game and pick a .sav file from the Chapter saves folder.
  3. The player copies your current saves to a backup file first (its name says "before import" and the date), then restarts the game at the title screen.
  4. Choose Load and file 1.

Importing replaces all three of the game's save files and the suspend data, so the backup is how you get your own saves back: import the backup file the same way. The saves are in this player's own format; other emulators cannot read them.

Each save holds the army of a test playthrough at that point, with the levels it earned. Most of that playthrough's battles were won quickly with a testing aid, so its army may be weaker than yours would be by then. The cheats can make up the difference.

The Cheats menu changes the game you are playing. Each cheat applies once per click and stays within the limits the game itself uses.

The console date

The Neo Geo Pocket Color has a clock, and the game uses it for one thing only: the two date secrets after the ending. By default the player gives the game your PC's date. Options > Console date can set it to 25 December or 6 June instead, so you can meet the Light Priest or the Curse Priest on any day. The choice is kept in zenobia.ini until you change it back to The PC's date.

9. About this guide

This is an unofficial fan guide for the English fan translation of Densetsu no Ogre Battle Gaiden: Zenobia no Ouji (Neo Geo Pocket Color, 2000). It is not affiliated with the owners of the game.

The facts in it were checked against the game itself: its scripts and data tables, and test runs of the translated game. Rules that could not be confirmed are left out or marked as unconfirmed.

Names and terms follow the translation. Characters and places that appear in the official English releases of the Ogre Battle series keep the names from those releases (for example Warren, Canopus, Lanselot, Gilbert, Lyon, Zenobia and Zeteginea); everything else is the translation's own choice.
